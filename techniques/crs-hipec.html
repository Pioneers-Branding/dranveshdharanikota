CRS-HIPEC Procedure Steps

A comprehensive two-phase treatment approach combining surgery with heated chemotherapy

1

Pre-operative Assessment

Comprehensive evaluation including imaging studies, laboratory tests, and multidisciplinary team consultation to determine candidacy.

2

Cytoreductive Surgery

Surgical removal of all visible tumor deposits from the peritoneal cavity, achieving complete or optimal cytoreduction.

3

HIPEC Administration

Heated chemotherapy perfusion at 42-43°C for 60-90 minutes to eliminate microscopic cancer cells.

4

Recovery & Monitoring

Intensive post-operative care with careful monitoring and comprehensive recovery support.

Treatment Indications

CRS-HIPEC is indicated for specific types of peritoneal surface malignancies

Peritoneal Carcinomatosis

Colorectal Cancer

Advanced colorectal cancer with peritoneal spread, offering hope for improved survival outcomes.

Ovarian Cancer

Primary or Recurrent

Advanced ovarian cancer with peritoneal involvement, particularly in recurrent disease settings.

Appendiceal Cancer

Pseudomyxoma Peritonei

Rare appendiceal cancers causing mucin accumulation in the peritoneal cavity.

Gastric Cancer

Peritoneal Metastases

Advanced gastric cancer with peritoneal dissemination in selected patients.

Advantages of CRS-HIPEC

Unique benefits of combining surgical cytoreduction with heated chemotherapy

Direct Drug Delivery

Chemotherapy is delivered directly to the peritoneal cavity, achieving higher local concentrations.

Hyperthermia Effect

Heat enhances drug penetration and cytotoxicity while being selectively toxic to cancer cells.

Reduced Systemic Toxicity

Limited systemic absorption reduces side effects compared to intravenous chemotherapy.

Synergistic Effect

Combination of surgery and heated chemotherapy provides superior outcomes.
